By the last decade, the models tell three different stories.

In 2091–2100, none of the 40 models average at least 3°C of warming under SSP1-2.6. Under SSP5-8.5, all 40 do. SSP2-4.5 is closest to an even split, with 22 models at or above 3°C and 18 below it.

Data and methods

We use global mean near-surface air temperature from CMIP6, compiled by Mathias Hauser at ETH Zurich. Each value is an average over the selected ten years, expressed in degrees Celsius above that model’s 1850–1900 historical average. SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, and SSP5-8.5 describe low, intermediate, and high emissions pathways.

We weight the 40 models equally and use one simulation per model for each pathway. The same models appear in every comparison. Counts describe this group of models under the specified pathways. The project CSV is a filtered and reformatted subset of the source.

Prototype writeup

What have we done so far?

We made six exploratory plots to compare projected warming across three CMIP6 pathways. We matched 40 models across the pathways and plotted their median warming for eight decades in D3. The decade slider highlights a period and updates the median and model count for each pathway. Readers can switch between 2°C and 3°C to see how threshold counts change for the same decade.

What will be the most challenging part to design and why?

The hardest part will be showing how much models disagree within a pathway while keeping the three pathways easy to compare. The median line gives each pathway a clear trajectory, but it leaves the distribution of individual model values out of view. For example, the last-decade SSP2-4.5 median is about 3.1°C, and 18 of the 40 models still average below 3°C. We want to test whether highlighting the same model across all three pathways lets readers explore that spread without obscuring the median trends.
